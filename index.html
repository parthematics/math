01 / The first experiment

Same circle. Different clock.

≈ 20 min in the lab

A particle speeds up around a circle. Does the circle become more curved? Make a guess, then play with the clock.

A circle, seen two ways

Same point, same circle, same arrow scale. Counterclockwise motion. Pausing freezes the picture; it doesn’t set the clock rate to zero.

The shape doesn’t know about your clock.

Speed measures distance per time. Curvature measures turning per distance. Change the clock and you change the journey, while the circle keeps its shape.

03 / Where this can take you

A path into curved spaces.

~24 weeks · 4 hours / week

A flexible route built around your engineering background. The first lab is here; the rest is a curriculum for guided lessons in chat. Move on when an idea sticks, not when a week ends.

00A first tasteWeek 1 · You are hereLAB AVAILABLE
Explore a curve, separate shape from speed, and use a short placement check to find the foundations you need. Read the full first lesson →
01The language of proofWeeks 2–5 · Proofs + linear algebra+
Definitions, quantifiers, counterexamples, vector spaces, inner products, and quadratic forms. Goal: write a short proof and explain the geometry behind a metric matrix.
02What “smooth” really meansWeeks 6–9 · Analysis + multivariable calculus+
Limits, derivatives as linear maps, the chain rule, local inverse functions, and arc length. Goal: connect a local linear approximation to a precise statement.
03The secret lives of curvesWeeks 10–12 · Curvature + torsion+
Regular curves, arc-length parameters, osculating circles, space curves, and torsion. Goal: derive curvature and explain why changing the clock does not change it.
04Life on a surfaceWeeks 13–16 · Tangent planes + metrics+
Charts, tangent planes, first and second fundamental forms, and Gaussian curvature. Goal: explain why a cylinder is intrinsically flat while a sphere is not.
05Geometry from the insideWeeks 17–21 · Geodesics + parallel transport+
Local isometries, covariant derivatives, geodesics, and parallel transport. Goal: find a geodesic and distinguish being locally straight from being globally shortest.
06When local becomes globalWeeks 22–24 · Gauss–Bonnet + your capstone+
Spherical triangles, Euler characteristic, and Gauss–Bonnet. Finish with a worked exploration of map distortion, cylinder geodesics, or spherical angle excess.
