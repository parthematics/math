Speed and curvature

Learn what changes when you move faster along the same curve.

One circle, two ways to describe motion

Velocity describes direction and speed. The unit tangent keeps the direction and has length 1.

Predict first: If the point moves twice as fast, which arrow will change?

What to notice

  • Doubling angular speed doubles velocity’s magnitude. The unit tangent still has length 1.
  • Curvature measures turning per distance traveled. Changing the clock leaves it unchanged.
  • A smaller circle has greater curvature: κ = 1/R.
Why does the clock cancel?

Write the circle as γ(t) = (R cos(ωt), R sin(ωt)). Its speed is Rω.

The unit tangent is T(t) = (−sin(ωt), cos(ωt)). It turns at a rate ‖dT/dt‖ = ω.

Distance traveled satisfies ds/dt = Rω. Turning per unit distance is therefore:

κ = ‖dT/ds‖ = ω / (Rω) = 1/R

This calculation assumes R > 0 and a constant ω > 0. In later lessons, we generalize to regular changes of parameter.

A circle’s radius and angular speed both double. What happens to speed and curvature? Explain your reasoning.

Check the explanation

Speed Rω becomes four times as large. Curvature 1/R is halved. The angular speed changes how fast you move; the radius determines how much the circle bends per unit distance.
